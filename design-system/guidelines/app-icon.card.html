Borrow the material, not the subject
Five rounds tried to draw what the app does — overlapping captures becoming one long image — and every one collapsed into a glyph that is already taken: copy, menu, download, hamburger, waveform, perforation, barcode, spool, gunsight. A three-stage verb does not survive compression into one static mark. What shipped instead takes the direction's material and says nothing about stitching: ink, one accent, ruled not shadowed, a print shop rather than a toolbar.

Full-bleed ink, three joins at unequal spacing, the middle one carrying --mark-flag — the uncertain seam. Unequal gaps are what stop it reading as a barcode; the off-centre accent gives the mark a top and a bottom. Geometry, the size ladder and the do-not-invert rule live on the AppIcon card under Marks.

crop marks — print-native and distinctive, but with a rule added it reads as a scanner viewfinder.
registration — the best idea of the five: literally how two impressions are aligned. Reads as a gunsight, and that cannot be designed away.
split — crisply on-palette, but a two-tone block belongs to any app.
spine — on-brand to a fault; reads as a reading app.
Rules paid for in failed rounds. Ink on paper, never paper on paper — --sheet on --paper is 1.1:1, because those tokens were contrast-solved for type on a ground, not a shape on one. · Nothing thinner than ~20px at 1024. · Judge at 120/76/60/40px, never at 1024 alone. · Perforations mean tear here and wide gaps mean separated: both invert the product. · A rectangle with one horizontal line has no silhouette — it becomes a document, a menu, or a numeral depending on how you nudge it.
