Borrow the material, not the subject
Four rounds tried to draw what the app does — overlapping captures becoming one long image. Every one collapsed into a glyph that is already taken: copy, menu, download, hamburger, waveform, perforation, barcode, spool. A three-stage verb does not survive compression into one static mark. So the icon takes the direction's material instead — warm stock, ink, one accent, square corners, ruled not shadowed, a print shop rather than a toolbar — and says nothing about stitching at all.
Proposed
Crop marks
A printer's crop marks denote a region selected out of something larger — which is what a capture is — so it carries the product without diagramming it. One paper rule crosses the field: a single join, drawn the way this direction draws joins. Marks are --mark-flag, the field is --ink. Light, dark and tinted are drawn from tokens, never filtered from one another; at night the desk darkens and the figure inverts.
Considered and rejected
registration
split
spine
offset
registration — the best idea here: it is literally how two impressions are aligned. Reads as a gunsight, and that cannot be designed away.
split — bold and crisply on-palette, but a two-tone block belongs to any app, and the ochre tinted variant goes blotchy.
spine — on-brand to a fault; reads as a reading app, and the blue band is decoration.
offset — the copy glyph, and the displacement looks like a rendering fault.
Rules paid for in failed rounds. Ink on paper, never paper on paper — --sheet on --paper is 1.1:1, because those tokens were contrast-solved for type on a ground, not a shape on one. · Nothing thinner than ~20 px at 1024, or the icon's content is exactly what disappears at 60. · Judge every candidate at 120 / 76 / 60 / 40 px, never at 1024 alone. · Perforations mean tear here, and wide gaps mean separated: both invert the product.
